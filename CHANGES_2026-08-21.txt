============================================================================
1. WHAT PROBLEM THIS PATCH TARGETS
============================================================================
Section 4.3 / 5.2 of the article identify spurious reflections at the
truncated ends of the leads (where the finite-length Complex Absorbing
Potential, CAP, replaces the physically infinite leads) as a leading cause
of the attenuated Aharonov-Bohm / Aharonov-Casher oscillation amplitude
compared to the analytical (transfer-matrix) result. The literature review
in Soluciones_propuestas ranked "discrete transparent boundary conditions
(DTBC)" (Akramov, Yusupov, Ehrhardt, Matrasulov 2026, arXiv:2608.05338;
Arnold & Ehrhardt 1999) as the highest-confidence fix: an EXACT, not
approximate, replacement for the CAP that eliminates reflection by
construction instead of absorbing it imperfectly.

============================================================================
2. WHAT WAS ACTUALLY IMPLEMENTED (and how it differs from full DTBC)
============================================================================
The full DTBC in the cited papers is a time-convolution (memory) kernel
that is exact for the ENTIRE wavepacket, for a general finite-difference
discretization of the continuous Schrodinger equation. Implementing that
kernel correctly from a paraphrase of a paper I could not fully verify
line-by-line within this run would have meant shipping a "reflectionless"
claim I could not stand behind — worse than not implementing it at all in
thesis-adjacent code. Instead, this patch implements something narrower
but rigorously self-derived and verified:

  EXACT SINGLE-SITE LEAD SELF-ENERGY BOUNDARY CONDITION (monochromatic /
  memoryless approximation to full DTBC).

  The leads in this code are already an exact uniform 1-D tight-binding
  chain (on-site energy 2*t_lead, hopping -t_lead, see
  build_single_ring_hamiltonian in tools.py) with dispersion
  E(k) = 2*t_lead*(1 - cos(k*delta_x)). For SUCH a chain, the retarded
  self-energy of the removed semi-infinite continuation beyond a single
  boundary site has a well-known closed form (standard tight-binding lead
  embedding, e.g. Datta, "Electronic Transport in Mesoscopic Systems"):

      Sigma(E) = -t_lead * exp(i * k(E) * delta_x)

  Adding Sigma(E_F) to the on-site energy of ONLY the outermost site of
  each lead makes that single site behave exactly as if the lead
  continued to infinity, FOR A WAVE AT ENERGY E_F. No finite absorbing
  ramp region and no extra lead padding are required, unlike the CAP.

  Limitation (stated explicitly, not hidden): this is exact only for the
  packet's central Fourier component. A Gaussian wavepacket has a finite
  momentum spread (Delta_k/k_F ~ 13% for the sigma=150 nm packets used
  here), so some residual reflection remains, scaling with that spread
  rather than with an arbitrary CAP ramp shape/strength/width. The full
  time-convolution DTBC (still open, see section 4 below) would remove
  that residual too.

3. FILES CHANGED
============================================================================

conductance.py (modified)
  - Added lead_wavenumber(p, energy_mev): solves the exact tight-binding
    dispersion E = 2*t_lead*(1-cos(k*delta_x)) for k.
  - Added lead_self_energy(p, k): closed-form Sigma(E) = -t_lead*exp(i k dx).
  - Added build_cn_matrices_with_transparent_bc(p, layout, energy_mev):
    builds the Crank-Nicolson A/B matrices with Sigma(E_F) embedded at the
    single outermost site of each lead (left_lead_sites[0] and
    right_lead_sites[-1], both spin components). This directly parallels
    the existing build_cn_matrices_with_cap for easy comparison.
  - Added run_transparent_conductance(...): mirrors run_cap_conductance's
    signature and CAPConductanceResult return type (same T/R/T+R/G
    bookkeeping), but accumulates absorption at the single self-energy
    site instead of integrating over a CAP ramp region. This makes the
    two methods directly swappable and comparable in existing scripts.
  - Nothing existing was removed or altered; run_cap_conductance and
    build_cn_matrices_with_cap are untouched, so all previously generated
    results/plots remain reproducible byte-for-byte with the old code path.

ab_ac_proof.py (modified)
  - simulate_G(...) gained two new optional keyword arguments:
    boundary="cap" (default, UNCHANGED behaviour, byte-identical to
    before) or boundary="transparent" (routes to the new
    run_transparent_conductance), and total_time_ps (was hard-coded to
    35.0, now a parameter defaulting to 35.0 so future experiments do not
    need to edit the function body).
  - Default behaviour is untouched: plot_ab_oscillations / AB and AC plots
    still use boundary="cap" unless a caller explicitly asks for
    "transparent". This was a deliberate choice, see section 4 below —
    switching the default would not currently improve the headline plots
    for reasons unrelated to the lead boundary, and I did not want to
    silently change reference figures without a matching fix.
  - Ran `python ab_ac_proof.py --quick --plots ab` end-to-end after the
    change to confirm no regression (see section 5, "Testing performed").

test_transparent_boundary.py (new file)
  - A focused, cheap, decisive benchmark that isolates JUST the lead
    boundary condition from the ring/junction physics. It builds a bare
    600-site spinless 1-D tight-binding chain (no ring, no junction) with
    the same t_lead/delta_x as the real leads, launches a Gaussian packet
    toward one end, and measures how much probability reflects back
    versus is absorbed, for three boundary treatments: "none" (hard-wall
    reference, sanity check), "cap" (the repo's existing CAP with its
    current default parameters: cap_fraction=0.20, cap_strength=2.0,
    cap_order=3), and "transparent" (the new self-energy boundary).
  - Run with: python test_transparent_boundary.py

============================================================================
4. TESTING PERFORMED AND RESULTS
============================================================================

(a) Isolated boundary-reflection benchmark (test_transparent_boundary.py),
    E_F = 4.19 meV (the article's default Fermi energy), 20 ps simulated
    (~1.6x the full-chain transit time):

        boundary          reflected fraction of injected probability
        ----------------  -------------------------------------------
        none (hard wall)  0.9980   (sanity check: ~all of it, as expected)
        cap (defaults)    0.0141
        transparent       0.0022

    -> the new boundary reflects ~6.3x LESS probability than the existing
       CAP at the article's reference energy. Repeating at other Fermi
       energies:

        E_F = 2.00 meV:  CAP 0.0488  vs  transparent 0.0019   (26x less)
        E_F = 4.19 meV:  CAP 0.0141  vs  transparent 0.0022   (6.3x less)
        E_F = 8.00 meV:  CAP 0.0013  vs  transparent 0.0008   (1.7x less)

    The improvement is largest at low energy (where the current CAP's
    fixed cap_strength=2.0 meV ramp is comparatively too abrupt for the
    slower/longer-wavelength packet) and shrinks but never reverses at
    higher energy. This confirms the self-energy boundary is a genuine,
    energy-robust improvement over the current CAP for the specific
    problem of lead-truncation reflection.

(b) Full ring + two leads + Y-junctions (the actual physical system),
    comparing run_cap_conductance vs run_transparent_conductance at
    Phi=0.5, alpha=20 meV*nm (V=0 everywhere, i.e. exactly the
    "transparent_ring_params" configuration ab_ac_proof.py already uses
    to validate against the analytical Buttiker formula):

        total_time_ps    CAP  T+R        transparent  T+R
        --------------   -----------     -----------------
        35               0.371           0.133   (still rising)
        70               0.377           0.214   (still rising)
        120              0.377           0.286   (still rising)
        250              0.380           0.351   (still rising, slowly)

    Both plateau far below 1 (T+R should equal 1 if all injected
    probability eventually leaves through one lead or the other) and the
    numerical G/G0 at Phi=0.5 (0.05-0.07) is nowhere near the analytical
    prediction of 1.67 even after 250 ps (~23x the nominal ring transit
    time), REGARDLESS of which boundary condition is used. This is the
    important, honest finding of this run: the reflections described in
    section 4.3/5.2 of the article are not (or not only) coming from the
    lead truncation that the CAP/transparent boundary treats. They are
    largely explained by a SEPARATE mechanism already noted in passing in
    ab_ac_proof.py's own comments: the Y-junctions are 3-port couplers
    with an intrinsic ~11% reflection each (topological, not a numerical
    artifact), and the ring can host long-lived quasi-bound resonances
    that trap probability circulating between the two junctions for much
    longer than the nominal single-pass transit time. Fixing the lead
    boundary cannot shorten that resonance lifetime.

    This means "Soluciones propuestas" solution #1 (DTBC / this lighter
    self-energy version of it) is a real, verified improvement for the
    problem it targets (lead-end reflection), but is NOT by itself
    sufficient to close the quantitative gap described in section 5.2 of
    the article. See "Problems still unsolved" below.

(c) Regression check: `python ab_ac_proof.py --quick --plots ab` was run
    end-to-end after the change (boundary parameter left at its default
    "cap") and completed normally, producing ab_ac_proof/ab_oscillations.png
    with no errors, confirming the new optional keyword arguments do not
    disturb the existing default code path.

5. PROBLEMS STILL UNSOLVED / SUGGESTED NEXT STEPS
============================================================================

1. Full ring oscillation amplitude is still far below the analytical
   Buttiker prediction (see 4(b) above), and this is now shown to be
   dominated by Y-junction quasi-bound trapping, not lead-end reflection.
   Candidates for the next scheduled run, roughly in order of how directly
   they follow from this finding:
     - Extend the exact self-energy idea from the lead ends to the
       Y-junctions themselves (an embedding self-energy for the "third
       arm" at each junction), which is closer to what solution #2
       (migrating to Kwant, arXiv:1309.2926) would give for free, since
       Kwant computes junction self-energies from the full lattice
       structure rather than only at chain ends.
     - Alternatively, switch to a stationary (energy-fixed) scattering
       calculation for the conductance instead of integrating a
       time-dependent wavepacket for a finite duration -- this sidesteps
       the "how long do I wait for quasi-bound resonances to decay"
       question entirely, at the cost of losing the time-dependent
       circuit-verification use case in section 3.4 of the article.
     - Verify independently (e.g. against a known closed-form S-matrix for
       a symmetric 3-port Y-junction) that the ~11% per-junction
       reflection value quoted in ab_ac_proof.py's comments is actually
       correct for THIS graph Hamiltonian's specific hopping ratios
       (t_lead vs t_ring, plus the existing junction_correction term in
       tools.py) rather than assumed from a generic continuum-limit
       argument.

2. The full time-convolution DTBC (exact for the whole wavepacket, not
   just its central Fourier component) described in
   Soluciones_propuestas_CAP_DTBC_QPC_2026-08-21.md as the top-ranked
   solution was intentionally NOT implemented this run (see section 2
   above for why: the risk of an unverified convolution-kernel formula in
   thesis-adjacent code outweighed the incremental gain over the
   monochromatic version implemented here, given the isolated benchmark
   already shows the monochromatic version helps substantially). If a
   future run has time to carefully re-derive or independently verify the
   kernel from Arnold & Ehrhardt (1999) / arXiv:2608.05338 against a known
   test case (e.g. compare its reflection at TWO different momentum
   components of a two-tone test packet, where the monochromatic version
   implemented here is expected to fail and the full DTBC should not),
   that would close this gap.

3. The QPC potential reconciliation problem (Ec. 3-26 gaussian vs Ec.
   1-25/1-26 parabolic, "Soluciones propuestas" solution #4) was not
   addressed this run; scope was kept to the reflection problem only.

4. gaussian_qpc_plots.py, six_conductance_plots.py,
   animate_conductance_vs_Uleads_UU_phi_so.py, caso_ejemplo.py and the old
   test.py were not touched or reviewed this run.
